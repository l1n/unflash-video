Unflash

Find the flashing in a video that can trigger photosensitive seizures, and take it out without wrecking the footage. Everything runs in your browser, on your GPU; nothing is uploaded anywhere.

  1. Open a video (an MP4), then Scan it. Every frame is decoded with WebCodecs and pushed through the detector as fast as your GPU allows. Unflash puts a numbered section around every problem it finds.
  2. Or just play it with live monitor on: the detector runs on every frame the player shows and the meter above the video tells you, as it happens, how much of the picture is flashing.
  3. Prepare a section to pull out its frames, then mark the frames you want gone (R/F) or held (E). The section is re-checked the instant you change anything, with nothing to render.
  4. Or let Suggest do it: keep-light and keep-dark work out a set of removals, reduce FPS thins the section down to a rate that cannot flash fast enough to fail.
  5. Export re-encodes the edited video in the browser and Verify re-scans the result.

Extended flashes are 5 seconds or more of flashing that meets every WCAG failure condition except the rate: it runs at the permitted speed (3 flashes/s) instead of above it. WCAG lets that through, but UK broadcast guidance doesn't and it does affect some viewers, so the default profile flags them as ordinary sections. Pick Exact WCAG only to ignore them. Stricter than WCAG already fails at that speed.

This reduces risk. It can't guarantee safety. It doesn't detect hazardous static patterns such as fine stripes, and individual sensitivity varies, so review the flagged sections yourself before you share anything.
